Understanding the model

Pay rise analysis evaluates a single annual salary review. Real pay rise = (1 + nominal pay rise) / (1 + inflation benchmark) − 1. Monthly and fortnightly increases are gross annual differences divided by 12 and 26; these are not take-home pay. Applying a rise to the projection assumes the same percentage repeats every year.

Nominal salary grows annually at your selected salary-growth rate. Real salary converts that amount into base-year purchasing power using cumulative CPI inflation. The historical CPI series changes when you change countries; currencies are labels, not converted exchange rates.

Required salary = base salary × cumulative CPI factor. Present value = selected nominal salary ÷ (1 + discount rate)years from base; this is a financial discounting measure, distinct from real purchasing power. For years before the base year, the inverse formula estimates a value compounded forward to the base year.

Tax calculation controls: NZ IETC is only applied when you expressly confirm eligibility, and Australian Medicare single-person low-income thresholds use an editable 2025–26 reference as a proxy until 2026–27 values are verified. An arithmetic reconciliation does not certify payroll accuracy. Employer-paid KiwiSaver/superannuation is not a deduction from the employee gross salary. NZ IETC eligibility guidance · ATO Medicare thresholds.

Data integrity: Historical annual CPI growth comes from the World Bank where available. Only completed calendar years with valid returned observations count as actual; a missing completed year remains historical estimated. Cumulative CPI is classified independently from annual CPI, and any forward result is labelled forecast. Any missing years (including future periods) use your assumed annual inflation rate; strict mode suppresses incomplete historical purchasing power. Assumed observations are labelled Assumption or Forecast. If the CPI API cannot be reached, strict historical comparisons remain unavailable; forecast-only comparisons can still use the user-selected future inflation assumption. Illustrative mode clearly identifies assumed missing historical rates. Salary growth, career events and historical salaries are user assumptions, not country statistics; taxes are estimates using selected rules or a user rate. FX rates are not modelled.

Release governance: v6.2 release candidate reviewed 21 November 2026 (NZ time). NZ/AU rate snapshot: 2026–27. Country-specific CPI must be checked on a live HTTPS deployment; refresh source data when using the model. Tax settings must be reviewed at least annually and after legislative changes.

Historical sensitivity: A salary entered against an earlier base year is treated as an assumed salary at that date. Salary growth is hypothetical across the whole comparison period. This dashboard is educational, uses a separate 2026–27 tax snapshot on a separately entered salary rather than historical tax law and does not constitute individual financial or tax advice.

Source: World Bank — Inflation, consumer prices (annual %) · World Bank Indicators API documentation. Tax references: NZ IRD, NZ ACC levy, Australian ATO. No sign-in, analytics, tracking or external JavaScript libraries.
